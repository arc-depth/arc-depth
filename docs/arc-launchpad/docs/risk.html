Risk disclosure

Don't put in money you can't afford to lose. Crypto assets are high risk, and you should be prepared to lose everything you put in. Read this before you launch, buy or trade.

On this page

  1. Crypto assets and memecoins
  2. Perpetual futures (leverage)
  3. The trading key on your device
  4. Smart contracts and cross-chain
  5. Models and data
  6. Regulation and tax

1. Crypto assets and memecoins

  • Prices can fall to zero, quickly. Memecoins usually have no use beyond entertainment.
  • Liquidity can disappear: you may not be able to sell at the price you see.
  • Anyone can launch a token. A token's name, picture or mascot says nothing about who is behind it.

2. Perpetual futures (leverage)

  • Leverage multiplies gains and losses. A small move against you can liquidate your position and take its whole margin.
  • With cross margin, one losing position can use the margin of your whole Hyperliquid account.
  • Funding is paid every hour between longs and shorts and can cost you while you hold a position.
  • Stop losses and take profits are market orders when triggered: in fast markets they can fill at a worse price, or a gap can jump past them.
  • Hyperliquid can auto-deleverage (ADL) profitable positions in extreme events, and its own risk rules, oracles and liquidity vault apply.
  • ARC PULSE does not run Hyperliquid. Your orders, margin and positions live on Hyperliquid; outages, bugs or rule changes there are outside our control.

3. The trading key on your device

To place orders without a wallet pop-up each time, the trade page can create a trading key in this browser, approved by your wallet. It can place and cancel orders for your Hyperliquid account but cannot withdraw or transfer funds. Anyone with access to this browser could use it to trade. You can remove it on the trade page or on Hyperliquid at any time.

4. Smart contracts and cross-chain

  • Our contracts are open source and verified but not audited; a bug could lose funds.
  • Cross-chain steps (relay.link, Circle CCTP, LayerZero) depend on those systems and can be delayed or fail.
  • Transactions are final once confirmed. Check addresses and amounts before you sign.

5. Models and data

Pulse HUD, Target Radar, Aim assist and the Time Machine use public data and statistical models. They describe what has happened and what could happen under assumptions; they cannot see news, hacks or decisions that have not happened yet. Their odds can be wrong. The Time Machine's fixed predictions are scored in public so you can judge it, but past scores do not guarantee anything.

6. Regulation and tax

Rules for crypto and derivatives differ by country and change. Some products may be unavailable or illegal where you live; it is your responsibility to check. Gains may be taxable.
